# ZCHF (Frankencoin) — Risk Report
**Elevated risk · 4.0/10**
*Stablecoin · Swiss francs · Ethereum + 7 chains · No issuer · ~CHF 34M · verified 2026-08-21*

> **This token is pegged to the Swiss franc, not the dollar.** If you price it in USD you will see
> it swing 10% or more and conclude it is broken. That is the dollar moving, not ZCHF. Every peg
> figure below is measured **ZCHF against CHF**. For a dollar-denominated portfolio, ZCHF is a
> currency position first and a credit position second, and the currency call is not what this
> report scores.

## Summary

Frankencoin is a borrowing protocol that issues Swiss francs on-chain. Anyone can lock up
collateral — bitcoin, staked ether, gold, tokenized shares — and mint ZCHF against it, the same
basic shape as MakerDAO or Curve's crvUSD. What makes it genuinely unusual is that **it uses no
price oracle anywhere.** There is no feed telling the protocol what your collateral is worth.
Instead, the borrower declares their own liquidation price when they open a position, and anyone
who thinks that price is a lie can **challenge** it by putting up the same collateral and forcing
an auction. Price discovery and liquidation are the same event.

There is also no issuer. A published legal assessment by LEXR Law Switzerland concludes ZCHF is
generated by a decentralised protocol "not centrally organised or controlled by any legal entity."
We checked that from the other direction: `owner()` and `admin()` both revert on the token
contract. There is no admin key, no freeze function, no custodian and no company that can be
subpoenaed. New minting rights arrive only through a 14-day public application that any 2% of
governance-token holders can veto. That is the entire control surface, and it is unusually clean.

The problem is not the design — it is what is behind it and how much of it there is. About **CHF
34.2M** of ZCHF is outstanding — CHF 30.3M on Ethereum plus CHF 3.9M bridged, mostly to Base and
Gnosis — against CHF 33.5M of borrower debt, and **roughly 31% of that debt is collateralised by
shares in two private Swiss companies**. Those shares do trade, in small size, on an on-chain OTC
venue — but the larger of them, the tokenized share register of an ICT company called Boss Info
AG, is **marked above the prices it actually changes hands at**, and its issuer's audited
consolidated accounts show **CHF 362,014 of total equity** behind a mark implying an CHF 86M
valuation. The first-loss capital standing behind the whole system is **CHF 3.81M**. Three
borrowers account for 77% of all debt; the largest holder of Boss Info shares — **52.8% of the
company** — is the same person borrowing against them; and the share contract is one where **a
single ordinary key can destroy the exact collateral backing four million francs of debt**. None
of it is hidden — all of it is readable on-chain, which is more than most stablecoins can say —
but it is a concentrated credit view on a handful of counterparties, not a cash-equivalent.

## Risk by axis

**Peg mechanism — 6.5.** The record is good and it is measured, not asserted. Against the Swiss
franc over the trailing year ZCHF traded between **0.9827 and 1.0151** — a worst deviation of
−1.73% — and closed below 0.99 on five days. That is a tighter band than crvUSD has held, on
one-sixth the supply, and it has been maintained for three years with **no redemption backstop at
all**. Nothing forces ZCHF back to par except arbitrage, the cost of borrowing, and the savings
rate. Holding that band without a redeem button is the single most impressive fact about the asset.

The oracle-free design deserves credit too. Because there is no price feed, there is no feed to
manipulate — an attack surface that has cost other lending protocols nine figures simply does not
exist here. Liquidation runs as a two-stage auction: a challenger posts collateral, and for a set
window anyone can buy that collateral at the borrower's stated liquidation price, which proves the
position sound and cancels the challenge. If nobody does, a Dutch auction opens at that price and
declines to zero, and the first bidder takes the collateral. Losses hit the borrower's own reserve
contribution first, then FPS equity, then other borrowers' reserves pro rata.

What keeps this off a 7 is that the auction starts from a price **the borrower chose**, not a market
price, and borrowers set it as high as the rules allow. Measured across all 71 open positions, the
book is collateralised **1.18x at those liquidation prices** — and on roughly 78% of the debt the
auction can fall less than 7% before proceeds stop covering the debt. The buffer is really the gap
between the declared liquidation price and the market price, which is comfortable today. It is not
a structural cushion.

**Backing — 4.0.** Start with the genuine strength: this is one of the very few stablecoins whose
backing can be reconstructed **entirely from public blockchain calls**, to the franc, with no
attestation, auditor or issuer dashboard in the loop. We did exactly that. Reserves in the equity
contract total CHF 12.44M against CHF 30.3M on mainnet, and the internal accounting reconciles
exactly. No fiat-backed stablecoin in our coverage can be verified this way.

Now the collateral. Roughly **31% of borrower debt is secured on shares in two private Swiss
companies** — BOSS (Boss Info AG, an ICT and ERP integrator) and DQTS (draggable quitt.shares) —
both tokenized through Aktionariat under Switzerland's 2021 DLT act, where the token *is* the entry
in the company's share register. Boss Info tokenized 100% of its capital in 2021: 10.65 million
shares, one share to one indivisible token. This is legitimate and well-documented, and an
oracle-free auction is arguably a *better* way to liquidate an illiquid share than a price feed
would be. But it means the collateral is an issuer-controlled register entry, not a bearer asset.
We verified on-chain what that implies: the BOSS token is an **upgradeable proxy** whose admin is a
single ordinary wallet, and a second ordinary wallet can `mint` new shares with no cap and `burn`
existing ones out of any address. Simulated against the live position backing 4,000,000 ZCHF, the
burn call succeeds for exactly that position's 800,000-share balance. Under Swiss corporate law
that control is *required* — an issuer must be able to record capital increases and cancellations —
but Frankencoin's contracts hold nothing that protects against it.

Concentration compounds it, and in a specific way. **Three borrowers hold 77% of all debt.** The
largest holder of BOSS — 5.6 million shares, **52.8% of the company** — is the same address that
owns three of the five BOSS positions and carries 24.7% of all ZCHF debt; between his wallet and
his posted collateral he controls **75% of Boss Info AG**. Collateral value and borrower solvency
are therefore one variable rather than two, and a forced sale would come precisely when the
company's controlling shareholder is distressed. The blocks are large relative to the companies
too: Frankencoin's contracts hold **22% of Boss Info AG** and **36% of quitt.shares**.

And the mark all of those figures rest on does not reconcile. The shares *do* trade — an on-chain
OTC venue printed **CHF 107,000 of BOSS across 14 trades in six weeks** — so this is not a market
that fails to exist. But the scale is the point: that is about **CHF 2,500 a day**, against a BOSS
position of 2.4 million shares. At the observed rate it would take **roughly seven years of flow
just to raise the CHF 6.9M of debt those shares secure**. DQTS looks deeper at first glance —
CHF 2.6M over five months — until you notice **99% of it was a single day** at a pre-agreed price
of exactly 8.000; strip that placement and five months of trading is CHF 25,000. And BOSS printed
at **6.90–8.20, clustering 7.30–7.55**, against the **8.072** mark Frankencoin displays, leaving
that position carried roughly **CHF 1.6–2.2M above realised prices**. And Boss
Info AG's audited consolidated accounts show **CHF 362,014 of total equity**, with 2025 net income
of CHF 57,318 after a CHF 3.99M loss the year before — against a mark implying an **CHF 86M**
market capitalisation. Frankencoin's stake is carried at CHF 19.19M; the attributable consolidated
book value is about **CHF 80,700**. Operating cash flow is healthy and a services business can
legitimately trade well above book, so this is not a valuation call — but nothing in the audited
record anchors a gap of that size. **That, not illiquidity, is why this axis is 4.0.**

Separately, 2.3% of supply is now issued against CHFAU, a BaFin-licensed franc stablecoin from the
Deutsche Bank/DWS/Galaxy venture AllUnity — better credit than a private share, but it inserts a
regulated, freezable counterparty into a system whose whole proposition is not having one, and its
approved ceiling is already **10M, a third of supply**, with no further vote required. In fairness
to the issuers: both share positions carry the protocol's **top-tier 40% borrower reserve**, DQTS
is marked *below* its own prints, an atomic on-chain settlement venue exists at all, and Swiss
company law limits how much of its own stock Boss Info could buy back even if it wanted to. The
realistic exit for a position this size is a negotiated block sale or a corporate transaction, not
a market. The score would move up if BOSS prints converge to its mark
or the tokenized-equity share falls; down if the CHFAU ceiling is used, if that share passes ~45%,
or if any issuer key is exercised against a live position.

**Liquidity — 5.5.** These are measured executable quotes, not pool TVL. On the main Uniswap V3
ZCHF/USDT pool, 100k ZCHF costs about 18bp, 400k costs **51bp**, and at roughly 450k the range is
exhausted and slippage jumps past 7%. A Curve pool against crvUSD adds 50k at 39bp and 200k at
378bp. Blended, **about CHF 500k clears same-day at roughly 61bp**, with a further ~$200k/day of
centralised volume and a small euro pair on Gnosis that is the only on-chain route to a European
fiat pair.

Three things cap the score. One venue holds ~80% of that capacity on about $1.3M of TVL, so this is
concentration, not depth. The depth is in tight concentrated ranges that a single LP can withdraw
in one block — re-read five hours apart, the deep Curve rung moved from −523bp to −378bp on a 7%
shift in pool balances. And only about **20% of ZCHF is genuinely floating**: 39% sits in the
savings module and 41% is protocol reserve, which is why the tradeable book is thin relative to the
market cap. Quote before you size; do not carry a number from this report.

One dated item to watch: a governance application filed 2026-08-20 would let anyone open a Uniswap
position posting only the dollar side, with the ZCHF side minted fresh — up to CHF 2.5M into that
same dominant pool. Its veto window closes around **2026-09-03**. If it goes live it could multiply
the main venue's depth, but that would be protocol-manufactured liquidity with an expiry date, not
organic LP interest.

**Issuer — 5.5.** There is no issuer, and that is both the score's floor and its ceiling. What it
buys is real: no bank, no custodian, no attestation to trust, no freeze function, no bankruptcy
estate, and a backing analysis that is fully verifiable from public data. `owner()` and `admin()`
revert on the token contract — there is nothing to compromise or subpoena. Three audit engagements
(ChainSecurity twice, Code4rena, BlockBite) and three years live with no depeg incident and no
reported bad-debt event.

What it costs is equally real: no redemption right against anyone, no recourse, no insurance, no
regulator, and nobody obliged to make a shortfall good. Loss absorption stops at whatever FPS
holders have voluntarily put up, which is **CHF 3.81M** — and note that this is the *shared* layer,
smaller than the CHF 12.44M sitting in the reserve contract, most of which is borrowers' own money
already earmarked against their own debt. Governance is also small enough to be cheap in both
directions: the 2% veto threshold is about **CHF 228k**, so blocking a dangerous new minter is
affordable, and so is blocking a good one. On a governance base this size, the veto is
simultaneously the security model and the concentration risk.

## Bottom line

Frankencoin is the credible on-chain Swiss franc, and its engineering is better than its score.
Oracle-free, adminless, transparent to the franc, with a three-year peg record tighter than much
larger competitors and no issuer to trust. If you want CHF exposure on-chain, this is the serious
option and there is no close second.

What you are actually underwriting is narrower than the design suggests: **CHF 3.81M of shared
first-loss capital behind CHF 33.5M of debt, roughly a third of it secured on private Swiss company
shares that are register entries under issuer key control, with 77% of the borrowing in three
hands.** It is a concentrated credit position on a small number of counterparties. Size it that way
— and for a dollar portfolio, remember the franc call dominates everything in this report.

**Not for:** anyone who needs to redeem at par, needs deposit protection, wants to move more than
about half a million francs quickly, or is treating this as a dollar cash equivalent.

**Watch items**
- Whether the `UniswapAmplifier` minter goes live after ~2026-09-03 — it would reshape the main pool.
- CHFAU-backed supply rising toward its approved 10M ceiling (a third of supply, no vote needed).
- Tokenized private-share collateral rising above ~45% of debt.
- Any change to the BOSS token's owner or proxy admin, or a rise in its share count.
- Boss Info OTC prints drifting further below the 8.07 mark its collateral is carried at.
- A DQTS drag-along vote opening — a 75% vote can force conversion of the shares held inside the position.
- FPS equity falling toward CHF 2M.
- ZCHF closing below 0.98 **against the franc** — never against the dollar.
